# When a UAE business has a contract dispute in India

A founder in Dubai pays an Indian supplier, but the goods arrive late or the invoices no longer match the order. Before commissioning a formal response, build a short record that lets a lawyer see what happened. Start with the signed agreement and amendments, then add the purchase orders, invoices, delivery records and correspondence that show when each side performed.

### Give counsel the contract and the chronology

Write one line per event, with a date and a reference to the supporting record. Keep the originals and distinguish what you personally observed from what the supplier or your team told you. If the contract contains a notice, governing law or dispute resolution clause, flag it for counsel to review. Save any notice already received with its envelope, delivery record or original email. Where a deadline is mentioned, tell counsel at the outset.

### Decide what outcome matters to the business

Getting a replacement delivery, collecting an unpaid balance and ending a supplier relationship each call for different decisions. Ask counsel what the first engagement covers, who will handle the Indian side and what might trigger additional work. Keep any UAE advice or representation in a separate scope with the appropriate professional.

# Indian property questions to resolve before you sell from the UAE

An inherited flat in India may look straightforward until a buyer asks who can sign, or a co-owner disagrees about selling. If you live in the UAE, make those questions the first order of business. A lawyer can assess them more efficiently when you provide the property location, the names on the records and a clear account of what you want to happen.

### Assemble a property file

Start with the ownership documents already available, plus any relevant will, correspondence, tax records or agreement between owners. List documents you know are missing. Tell counsel whether anyone occupies the property, whether there is an existing dispute, and whether somebody in India has already been appointed to act for you.

### Check what you are being asked to sign

If a proposed sale or transfer involves a power of attorney, ask Indian counsel to review its purpose and wording before you arrange any attestation. The Dubai consulate lists power-of-attorney attestation as a service. Ask separately whether the document will suit your transaction and what further steps may be required in India. # Planning your transport around an Indian legal appointment

When a legal matter affects travel dates, book around confirmed appointments rather than a tentative timetable. If your next step is a document attestation in the UAE, locate the appointment venue and check the current instructions before arranging transport. If a lawyer expects you in India, ask for the city, date and reason for attending before buying a ticket.

### Separate the UAE errand from the India trip

Keep action items in two columns. Under UAE, put confirmed appointments or document collection. Under India, list anything counsel says requires attendance there. If an appointment changes, this separation makes it easier to see which booking you need to adjust. Leave enough time for parking, building access and any separate appointment on the same day.

Ask about change terms before reserving transport for fixed dates. Carry only the records you need for that appointment and keep a secure backup. # What to collect when a project in India goes off scope

If you are overseeing a property fit-out or technical installation in India from the UAE, a dispute may start with a practical question. Was the change authorised? Did the installed work match the specification? Was the invoice for work already completed? Give an adviser enough material to distinguish those issues.

### Organise the baseline and the changes

Collect the signed scope, drawings, specifications, payment milestones and change approvals. Save dated inspection reports and original photos with any location or item reference you already have. Keep the contractor’s explanation alongside your own records. A short table with the agreed item, observed condition, relevant date and supporting file is easier to review than a long chat export.

### Separate technical assessment from legal advice

A technical specialist can comment on the condition or scope of work. Indian counsel can assess which contractual issues matter and what further evidence is useful. If there is an immediate safety concern, address it through qualified local personnel and document what was done.

# Before booking a trip to India for a legal matter

A court update, a document signing and a property visit can each change where you need to be and for how long. Start with the person responsible for the appointment in India. Ask them to confirm the city, venue, dates, original documents needed and whether your attendance is required before choosing flights.

### Build the itinerary around the least flexible appointment

If you have appointments in more than one city, mark which dates are fixed before booking connections. Allow time for the airport transfer and check-in at unfamiliar venues. Ask what happens if the appointment moves before paying for a restrictive fare, and check the conditions for any hotel booked around it.

### Keep documents separate from the booking record

Keep a private list of original documents you need, and check whether the receiving office expects translations, copies or other preparation. Give a travel adviser only the information needed to arrange travel. Direct questions about attestation and signing to the relevant authority and your lawyer.
